Interface 03 · Conditional markets

Two futures.
One decision to make.

Futarchy does not ask whether a proposal is good. It asks what a chosen measure of success will look like if the proposal passes, and what it will look like if it fails — then lets the two prices argue. The market supplies information to the decision; it does not take the decision.

How the two prices are constructed

The same underlying pair, split into two conditional branches. Neither branch is a vote — both are forecasts of a measurable number.

Step 1

Pick the measure

The measure must be observable, dated, and outside the control of whoever benefits from the outcome. Here: the GNO/wstETH ratio, read 30 days after trading closes.

Step 2

Split the collateral

One unit of collateral becomes one YES token and one NO token. Trading the pair is trading the two futures against each other, not betting on a yes-or-no event.

Step 3

Compare, then decide

If the pass-branch price is higher, the market expects the proposal to do better than not passing it. The DAO still votes — with a price attached to each future.
