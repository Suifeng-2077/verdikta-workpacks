Interface 05 · Risk

How risky, according to the market.

A single yes-or-no price tells you almost nothing about how much risk you are carrying. This surface asks the market the same question about every protocol at once — the annual probability of default — and puts the answers side by side, so the comparison is the reading.

One position, twelve estimates. Posting collateral once is what makes the ranking comparable — the market is forced to price all twelve against the same budget.

Reading it

Spread, not level

The useful output is the distance between protocols, not the absolute number. A desk sizing exposure cares whether Spark is four times Aave's implied default probability, and that comparison only exists because both were priced on one book.

Who moves the number

Agents, mostly

Eighty-three percent of flow on this board is automated: agents that monitor governance forums, oracle feeds and liquidity depth and reprice continuously. Humans set the position sizes; machines keep the estimates current.

Settlement

What counts as default

The resolution clause names the triggering events — a shortfall in redemptions, a governance freeze past the stated window, an oracle failure that halts withdrawals. It is written before the market opens and it names who decides.

Design note. This interface avoids the gauge-and-speedometer vocabulary that risk products default to. A ranked board with a numeric colour scale answers the actual question — compared to what? — and keeps twelve estimates readable on a phone without horizontal scrolling.
